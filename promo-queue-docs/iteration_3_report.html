Which system should own the promo queue?

The evidence favors one client-side visibility arbiter, bounded remote queue policy, and RMF campaign candidates where remote content adds value. That is a proposed layered contract—not a decision and not an RMF-versus-queue compromise.

The question and who is judging it

Not “is RMF good?” The choice is which system schedules, arbitrates, controls, and measures promos without forcing desktop to discard shipped work.

Mobile

A · Replace

RMF is the queue

All promos become messages; RMF gains arbitration, frequency, surfaces, events, and measurement.

B · Extend

Client queue

Client policy engine owns every promo; RMF remains an observed/registered content input.

C · Proposed

Layered contract

RMF supplies campaign candidates; the client queue alone grants visibility now.

B+ · Control

Remote queue policy

Client arbitration plus bounded remote kill, priority, cooldown, cap, and expiry.

C with the B+ policy contract. First ship the narrow iteration-1 NTP/modal permit seam without extracting PromoService. If C is approved, build the client arbiter, add bounded remote controls, bridge RMF candidates through native surfaces, and only then price per-surface RMF scheduling or generic modal rendering. iteration_3_findings.md §0

05

The proposed C contract

Campaign availability and visibility permission are different decisions. The contract makes one owner explicit for each.

RMF

Campaign candidate

  • Remote copy, localization, imagery
  • Coarse local targeting
  • Campaign activation/kill
  • messageID telemetry identity
  • Candidate add/change/remove
→
Queue

Visibility permit

  • Priority, severity, context
  • Pacing, cooldown, caps, expiry
  • Pending state + retry on blocker clear
  • Onboarding/deep-link/essential rules
  • promoKey history identity
  • Admit, delay, ineligible, unsupported
Native adapter

Immediate truth + render

  • Re-check local eligibility
  • Idempotent show/hide
  • Consent/OS/StoreKit/runtime actions
  • Confirm actual visibility
→
Accounting

Exactly-once outcome

  • One actual shown event
  • Dismiss/action outcome
  • Queue delay reason
  • Idempotency by promo/message/presentation

Identity rule

One bridge per native surface; one queue identity per canonical promo/campaign—not one queue registration per localized RMF object. promoKey owns long-lived policy/history; messageID owns RMF campaign telemetry. Unknown IDs fail closed. iteration_3_findings.md §C.2

RMF obligations

Publish candidates per surface; preserve delayed state; provide stable IDs/version; stop global surface monopoly before relying on independent remote surfaces; do not author unvalidated queue policy; version-gate new matchers/actions/surfaces.

RemoteMessagingStore.swift:92-125,443-506 · JsonToRemoteMessageModelMapper.swift:415-423
Queue + adapter obligations

Return admitted/delayed/ineligible/unsupported; persist delayed candidates; retry them when blockers clear and on the next relevant trigger; emit actual outcome once; revoke permits safely; avoid starvation; report delay reasons. The adapter never renders without a permit and keeps native safety effects local.

PromoService.swift:322-330,488-510,621-649 · iteration_3_findings.md §C.3
Top failure modes and defenses

Starvation: per-surface candidates and diagnostics. Priority conflict: RMF order selects candidate availability only. Double pixels: one coordinator/idempotency key. ID churn: stable promoKey. Old client: fail closed/min version. Offline kill: honest delivery semantics. Render race: versioned permit and visibility confirmation. Unsafe policy: narrowing-only schema and compiled bounds. Platform drift: registry plus conformance reports.

Why the current ExternalPromo seam is transitional

macOS registers separate NTP and tab-bar RMF ExternalPromos. They control their own visibility and ignore queue cooldown; the queue reacts and may retract internal promos. The final C seam reverses authority: RMF requests admission and the surface waits for a permit. PromoServiceFactory+RemoteMessage.swift:24-46 · Promo.swift:97-124 · PromoService.swift:431-457

06

Migration narratives and scoped work

The options are also sequences. The targeted iteration-1 permit seam is evidence, not a pre-commitment to the client-queue option.

A

Replace after parity

Build scheduler, policy, history, events, surfaces, metrics, tooling, and offline behavior; reconcile all 74 reported names, retire stale/non-promos, and migrate active units; replace/wrap desktop; build Android. Keeping the queue as arbiter is C.

B

Extract, adapt, then register

Build the Apple engine and real iOS provider/lifecycle adapters, complete pending/retry and policy, migrate private priority chains, retain RMF input, align desktop by spec, and build the Android twin.

C

Bridge in waves

If approved, build the client-arbiter foundation; stable IDs + bridge; seven headers/cards; two attention surfaces; two modal prototypes; class-(c) visibility adapters; optional scheduler later.

B+

Control before content

Three-promo pilot, then validated manifest. Content can stay native or use RMF independently. Compiled bounds remain authoritative.

07

Decision tree for every new promo

Formalizes the reported “why not RMF?” ladder without turning “RMF percentage” into the outcome metric.

1

Is it actually a promo?

Transactional alerts, utilities, and persistent IA use their native contract; publish visibility if collision-capable.

2

Does off-the-shelf RMF fit content, surface, action, targeting, and latency?

Use an RMF candidate plus queue admission.

3

Only a small privacy-clean matcher/action missing?

Use the cross-platform proposal process, ship vocabulary, then configure.

4

Can product simplify the special requirement?

Record the latency/targeting behavior deliberately dropped.

5

Must rendering or immediate eligibility remain native?

Use the native-surface/RMF hybrid bridge.

6

No remote-content value?

Use a queue-registered InternalPromo plus bounded B+ kill/policy.

7

Persistent Settings information architecture?

Keep local unless a time-bounded campaign requirement is explicit.

8

Bespoke and unqueued?

Not an allowed endpoint; escalate a documented exception through the AOR.

The escalation ladder and enforcement intent are reported (Asana), not code-verifiable. iteration_2_research.md:31-35,71-83
